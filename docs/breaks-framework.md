# Breaks Framework (Draft)

This working document lays out a practical framework for writing breaks / rave breaks / trance breaks. It focuses on three pillars—sample selection, synth selection, and song structure—so you can sketch tracks quickly and then refine with detailed sound design later.

## 1. Sample Selection

### Drums
- **Breakbeat bed:** Start with a classic amen, think, or hotpants break at 166–174 BPM. Low-pass the original to tuck it under fresher hits, and chop individual slices so you can rearrange fills.
- **Layering strategy:** Layer state kicks and snares with punchy one-shots to reinforce transients. Keep the break crunchy, but make sure modern layers occupy clean spectral lanes (kick <120 Hz, snare 180–220 Hz fundamental, airy hats 10 kHz+).
- **Ghost notes & swing:** Program ghost hits from the break slices, dropping velocity 40–55 so the groove keeps breathing. If the main break is straight, add subtle MPC-style swing (55–58%).

### Percussion & FX
- **Top loops:** Use short shuffled hat loops or shaker textures to drive momentum; high-pass units so they never fight the break.
- **Impact chain:** Keep a folder of risers, white-noise hits, and vinyl spins. Reuse per-project to keep the sonic palette cohesive.
- **Vocal hits:** Chopped rave vocals ("come on", "feel it") act as extra snares or fills. Formant-shift for variety but bounce everything to a common reverb bus to glue.

## 2. Synth Selection (Korg M1 + MicroKorg)

### Korg M1
- **Stabs & chords:** Use the Organ2, Lore, or Universe presets as harmonic material. Low-pass to 10 kHz and add chorus for width. Program chords as short, gated stabs for the intro and stretch them with more release for breakdowns.
- **Bass:** M1 Fingered Bass with a touch of drive sits well under breakbeats. Automate filter cutoff to follow chord energy.
- **Pads:** Layer M1 Atmosphere patches with subtle reverse reverb for breakdown swells.

### MicroKorg
- **Leads:** Use DWGS waveforms for classic trancey leads. Patch recipe: saw+DWGS mix, 24 dB filter, slight resonance, envelope with fast attack/decay and sustain around 30%. Add synced delay to create repeating hooks.
- **Arps & plucks:** Program the arpeggiator with 1/8th gate and 75% gate time. Choose square wave, short decay, no sustain for rhythmic plucks that answer the main lead.
- **FX sweeps:** Noise oscillator through band-pass filter with slow LFO sweep. Record long takes and chop into rises/falls.

## 3. Song Structure

### Hook Strategy
- Introduce a teaser hook at bar 17: a filtered version of the main lead layered with vocal chop hits.
- Full hook lands after first breakdown (bar 65–81). Double it with MicroKorg arp for a stacked chorus feel.

### Melodies & Chord Progressions
- Use a four-chord loop (e.g., Am–F–C–G) for emotional pull. Keep M1 stabs voicing spread across octaves to leave midrange space for leads.
- Lead motifs should be 1–2 bars, repeated with small variations (pitch bend up 2 semitones, or re-trigger at 16th note offset).
- Balance diatonic movement with sampled vocal shouts on the "and" of 2/4 for call-and-response.

### Build Ups
- Bar 33–48: automate low-pass filters upwards on the M1 pads while increasing breakbeat slices density.
- Create tension with 1-bar snare rolls every 4 bars; shorten decay to make final roll razor tight.
- Noise risers from MicroKorg plus uplifter FX from samples should peak right before the drop, then hard mute for half a bar to let impact breathe.

### Break Downs
- Strip to pad + vocal atmosphere for 8 bars. High-pass the break to 400 Hz and drench in reverb so the contrast with the drop feels massive.
- Bring back chord progression with extended release and add subtle MicroKorg plucks reintroducing rhythm.
- Use kickless, sub-only swells in bars 57–64 to foreshadow the drop melody.

### Arrangement Template (166–174 BPM)
1. **Intro (bars 1–16):** Break + filtered stabs, FX swells.
2. **Teaser (17–32):** Add vocal chops, MicroKorg plucks, rising energy.
3. **Build (33–48):** Snare rolls, filter sweeps, density ramp.
4. **Breakdown (49–64):** Pads, vocal air, minimal drums.
5. **Drop/Hook (65–96):** Full drums, main lead, bass.
6. **Mid-section (97–128):** Variations, secondary breakdown, re-drop.

This framework is intentionally modular—swap chord progressions or lead timbres, but keep the skeleton so tracks stay club-ready. Future revisions should capture mix references and detailed automation moves.
